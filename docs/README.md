# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

## Overview
OctoAcme uses a lightweight, cross-functional project management model that moves work through initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective stages. The goal is to turn ideas into measurable outcomes while keeping roles, responsibilities, and communication clear across the project lifecycle. These practices help teams align on scope, manage dependencies, reduce risk, and maintain quality from kickoff through post-release improvement.

## Project Management Process Summary
- Initiation: Validate the problem, confirm stakeholders, define success metrics, and decide whether a project should proceed.
- Planning: Align scope, backlog, milestones, dependencies, and Definition of Done so delivery can begin with a clear plan.
- Execution: Run standups, manage priorities, track progress with project boards, and keep delivery moving through clear ownership and structured review.
- Release and deployment: Verify readiness, deploy through controlled checkpoints, and communicate outcomes to stakeholders.
- Retrospective and continuous improvement: Capture lessons learned, assign action items, and turn improvements into ongoing process changes.

This model emphasizes customer value, iterative delivery, clear ownership, data-informed decisions, and psychological safety. It relies on shared artifacts such as the project one-pager, backlog, risk register, milestone plan, and retrospective notes to keep work transparent and aligned across teams.

## Roles and Working Rhythm
OctoAcme emphasizes clear role ownership. Product and project leads define outcomes and direction, while developers, QA, and stakeholders contribute to delivery, validation, and decision-making. The operating rhythm includes standups, weekly alignment, sprint or milestone reviews, and structured escalation when risks or blockers emerge.

## Quality and Assurance Practices
Quality is built into the workflow through acceptance criteria, CI checks, security scanning, PR review requirements, and testing at unit, integration, and smoke-test levels where relevant. Release readiness depends on passing validation, documented rollback plans, and post-deployment verification to reduce operational risk and support continuous improvement.
