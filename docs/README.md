# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

## Overview
OctoAcme uses a lightweight, cross-functional project management model that moves work through initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective stages. The goal is to align business needs, product outcomes, delivery execution, and quality standards while keeping stakeholders informed and work visible throughout the project lifecycle.

## Project management process summary
- Initiation: validate the problem, confirm stakeholders, define success metrics, and decide whether to proceed with planning.
- Planning: align scope, backlog, milestones, dependencies, and the definition of done so delivery can begin with shared expectations.
- Execution: track progress through standups, sprint planning, risk management, and dependency management with clear ownership across the team.
- Release and deployment: verify readiness, deploy through controlled checkpoints, and communicate the outcome and any follow-up actions.
- Retrospective and improvement: capture lessons learned, convert them into action items, and use them to improve future project delivery.

## Roles and responsibilities
OctoAcme’s project model emphasizes clear ownership across functional roles. Product leaders define outcomes and prioritize work, project managers coordinate delivery and communication, developers implement and test features, QA validates quality and acceptance criteria, and stakeholders provide input, approvals, and business context. These roles work together to reduce ambiguity and keep work moving toward measurable outcomes.

## Communication and quality practices
OctoAcme relies on regular communication rhythms, including daily standups, weekly delivery updates, milestone reviews, and escalation paths when blockers arise. Teams maintain a single source of truth for status, risks, and decisions, and they document issues, dependencies, and follow-ups in project artifacts such as the backlog, risk register, and release notes. Quality is built into the process through clear acceptance criteria, automated CI checks, testing standards, security scanning, and manual QA when needed.
